# PACT ATLAS — THE PRODUCT FILM
## Definitive Codex production direction · 14 November 2026

**STATUS: EXECUTE. Codex owns filming, motion design, editing, voice, sound, captions, exports and quality control.** Do not return a storyboard as the final deliverable. The final deliverable is a working, exceptionally crafted product film with genuine product footage. This brief supersedes the older, proof-heavy demo outline in CODEX_DEMO_HANDOFF_2026-11-14.md.

### 1. Creative mandate

Make a premium, motion-designed **product launch film** that sells PACT Atlas as a complete product, not a collection of transactions and not a generic website tour. It must show a visitor how PACT works, what they can do, why its conditional-funding architecture matters, and what the outcome looks like.

**Core product promise:** PACT Atlas coordinates community funding when projects depend on each other. It allows supporters and organizers to define funding conditions, explore a connected coalition, preview valid allocations, and inspect independently verifiable NEAR outcomes. The same pledge cannot be spent twice.

**Key experience arc:** Discover → Understand → Create → Model dependencies → Connect/review → Follow outcomes → Verify.

**North-star test:** Someone seeing the film for the first time should know what PACT Atlas is, why it exists, how a person uses it, how it differs from normal crowdfunding, and where to try the product, without opening the README.

Motion design must make the actual product **easier** to understand; it must never replace product reality with invented feature screens.

### 4. Narration — professionally polish, then time to exact edit

Use the following as the substantive first-pass script. Refine rhythm and word count against actual captured footage; do not invent features or claims. Keep the message in natural, simple English, approximately 130–145 spoken words/minute.

> Most crowdfunding works one project at a time.
>
> But what if one project only makes sense when another succeeds? And every supporter has conditions on where their contribution can go?
>
> Meet PACT Atlas. Conditional community funding, built on NEAR.
>
> Explore public campaigns, understand their rules, and follow how each agreement progresses.
>
> Or create your own. Define the projects, set their funding goals, add supporters, and describe which outcomes must happen together.
>
> PACT turns those commitments into a coordinated funding plan. It checks the dependencies, assigns each pledge only once, and shows whether the complete agreement can work.
>
> In the Coalition Atlas, the connections become visible. Beta depends on Alpha. Both have to fit before the agreement can activate.
>
> Supporters can inspect the terms, connect a NEAR wallet, and review the action they are being asked to approve.
>
> Now look at an actual campaign. Beta's restricted contribution arrived first. Alpha's followed. The contract accepted the valid allocation, activated both projects, and recorded both beneficiary payouts.
>
> In another campaign, the funding agreement expired and the contributor received a refund.
>
> Every outcome is visible in the activity history. And PACT lets anyone check the original result against NEAR.
>
> Discover campaigns. Build agreements. Understand the conditions. Verify the outcome.
>
> **PACT Atlas. Funding that respects the whole agreement.**

### 7. Animation direction — exact grammar

- **Scene changes:** use meaningful match cuts and connected spatial morphs. For example, a dependency line between Alpha and Beta becomes the line/edge in the real Coalition Atlas; a funding card becomes the editor's real campaign panel; receipt lines align to the live Verify screen.
- **Keyframe behavior:** 8–12 frames for purposeful small UI reveals, 12–20 frames for a short focus push, 20–30 frames for wider conceptual morphs; ease-out for entrance and restrained ease-in-out for camera. Use no elastic bounce. Numbers may count up only when the matching data is authentic.
- **Scale and motion:** restrained pseudo-3D camera and soft drop shadow, 1.015–1.09× zooms for clarity; an occasional stronger diagram move is allowed if it helps comprehension. Keep UI type sharp at the output resolution.
- **Typography:** tiny supporting labels only when legible. Main lines 50–95 px in 1920 width, UI-dependent labels no smaller than roughly 26 px when displayed full frame. Maximum one conceptual headline at a time. Do not pile labels on top of the interface.
- **Composition:** live screenshots should occupy most of the frame, not a tiny browser window surrounded by empty gradients. Use full-bleed editorial compositions, balanced negative space, and consistent frame hierarchy. Reveal information in the order people need to understand it.
- **Depth:** subtle parallax between abstract dependency lines and the product card surface, never a fake 3D blockchain world. Copper accent used only to signify the active relationship, constraint, or successful transition.
- **Transitions:** no arbitrary page wipes, stock title animations, floating coins, excessive motion blur, random sparkles or 100 different easing curves.
- **Accessibility:** no prolonged strobe, no flashing more than three times a second, no flickering text, readable safe-area captions and meaningful color contrast.

### 8. Audio and editorial finishing

- Voice: adult, calm, articulate, confident, not overdramatic, with natural pauses after each major idea. Generate/record the voice first and cut motion to the spoken beats; do not jam 200 words into 75 seconds.
- Music: subtle original or legally licensed electronic/ambient pulse; modern, hopeful, restrained, with a lift at the Coalition Atlas reveal and proof payoff, and a clean musical close. No recognizable copyrighted music or unauthorized samples.
- Sound design: original restrained ticks for selected nodes, a subtle connection sound for dependency formation, short soft whooshes for purposeful spatial moves, tonal confirmation at the real activated result. No button-click sound on every interaction.
- Mix: clear spoken voice remains dominant; aim for integrated approximately -14 LUFS stereo, peaks under -1 dBTP, use ducking during narration. Verify on laptop speakers and mobile at low volume.
- Master: 1920×1080 at 30 fps (or true captured 60 fps only if source and editor support it), H.264 high profile, yuv420p, AAC stereo 48 kHz, sane bitrate, no black bars or frame glitches.
- Captions: precise manual or verified timed subtitles. Captions must remain readable on X, inside 10% side and bottom safe areas, two lines maximum, and should not cover key UI.
- Thumbnail: real PACT UI crop integrated with the graph motif, headline preferably "**CONDITIONAL FUNDING. ACTUAL OUTCOMES.**" or similarly clear product-focused wording.
